| Field test | How to run it | What counts as a pass |

## The Route That Reaches Apex Hatches Faster

Big hatches do not come from standing around hoping. They come from cycling eggs faster than the other five riders in your server. The first hour has a shape, and the riders who respect it end a session with two or three dinosaurs instead of one.

| Phase | Time budget | Primary goal | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Drop-in | 0–3 min | Learn controls, scout a small radius | First egg spotted |
| First hatch | 3–8 min | Hatch and equip immediately | You're riding |
| Speed upgrade | 8–25 min | Chain harder-to-reach eggs, mutate once | Better mount |
| Collection push | 25–60 min | Optimize a repeatable lap | Two or three dinos |

Three habits separate fast riders from slow ones. First, treat the map as a bus route with predictable stops rather than a playground to wander. Second, mount the instant a hatch finishes — an unclaimed dinosaur on the ground is a free pickup for anyone else in the server. Third, only dismount near cover or a known spawn, because every dismount resets your momentum.

## Mutation: When a Big Mount Earns the Cycle

Mutation boosts speed according to the official description, and that is all it says. It does not say by how much, how long it takes, or whether it stacks. That means your judgment call is purely practical: does the mutated dinosaur move visibly faster than it did before?

Run the cycle on the best mount in your roster and nowhere else. Mutating a starter right after hatching it is the classic beginner trap, because the speed lift is smaller than the time you could have spent finding a rarer egg. If you genuinely cannot tell whether a mutation did anything, it was not worth the cycle on that dinosaur — bank the next attempt for something stronger.
